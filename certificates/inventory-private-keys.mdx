KryptoVault Certificate Inventory provides a centralized source of truth for certificates managed across the platform. It consolidates certificates discovered, imported, enrolled, generated, or renewed and keeps their trust, lifecycle, issuer, store, and cryptographic context connected in one place.

<Info>
  Certificate Inventory represents the managed certificate estate in KryptoVault. It is where certificate assets move from being individually identified to being continuously governed.
</Info>

## Certificate sources

Certificates can enter the inventory through different lifecycle paths.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Discovered" icon="radar">
    Certificates identified through Network, Domain, Cloudflare, or other supported discovery methods.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Imported" icon="file-import">
    Certificates brought into KryptoVault from an external source or existing certificate environment.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Enrolled or Issued" icon="file-signature">
    Certificates created through KryptoVault CSR, issuance, or enrollment workflows.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Renewed" icon="rotate">
    Replacement certificates created as part of the managed renewal lifecycle.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Regardless of source, certificates are brought into the same inventory model so that monitoring and governance remain consistent.

  <Card title="Certificate Store" icon="folder-tree">

## Trust and chain validation

KryptoVault evaluates certificate trust to help determine whether a certificate can be reliably used within its intended trust model.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Chain validation">
    Evaluates whether the certificate can be linked through its issuer chain to an expected trusted authority.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Issuer classification">
    Provides visibility into whether a certificate is associated with a public or private certificate authority environment.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Revocation readiness">
    Certificate validation can consider available revocation mechanisms such as CRL and OCSP information.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

Trust assessment helps identify incomplete chains, untrusted issuers, or certificates that require remediation.

## Certificate lifecycle

KryptoVault manages certificates as lifecycle assets rather than static records.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Discovered or created">
    The certificate first enters KryptoVault through discovery, import, enrollment, or issuance.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Managed">
    The certificate becomes part of the centralized inventory and is associated with its issuer, store, trust, and cryptographic context.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Monitored">
    KryptoVault monitors certificate validity, cryptographic posture, and operational risk throughout its active lifetime.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Renewed or revoked">
    Lifecycle operations can replace or revoke the certificate according to operational and security requirements.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Retired">
    Certificates that are no longer required can leave the active lifecycle while retaining the required operational traceability.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Expiration management

KryptoVault continuously evaluates certificate validity to help identify certificates that have expired or are approaching expiration so that renewal or replacement can be planned before service availability is affected.

<Warning>
  Expiration risk can extend beyond a single asset when the same certificate is used across multiple applications, stores, or environments.
</Warning>

## Post-quantum readiness

KryptoVault can assess certificate cryptography from a post-quantum readiness perspective.

This helps organizations understand which parts of the current certificate estate rely on classical cryptography and where future migration to quantum-resistant technologies may be required.

<Info>
  Post-quantum assessment supports cryptographic inventory and migration planning by identifying dependencies before a broader PQC transition.
</Info>

## Certificate risk intelligence

Certificate Inventory contributes to KryptoVault's broader certificate-risk model by correlating trust, validity, cryptography, and lifecycle information.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Trust risk">
    Identifies certificates with invalid or incomplete trust relationships.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Expiration risk">
    Highlights expired certificates and certificates approaching the end of their validity period.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Cryptographic risk">
    Identifies weak public-key or signature technologies and certificates that may not meet defined cryptographic expectations.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Revocation risk">
    Provides visibility into certificates that do not expose expected revocation-checking mechanisms.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Usage risk">
    Helps identify conditions such as self-signed certificates, certificate reuse, or certificate lifespans outside expected policy.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Governance and traceability

Certificate Inventory is governed through KryptoVault's role-based access model and operational audit capabilities.

Organizations can restrict sensitive lifecycle and cryptographic operations while allowing appropriate certificate visibility for security, PKI, infrastructure, and application teams.

KryptoVault maintains lifecycle and operational traceability so teams can understand how certificates entered the platform, how they changed, and which actions were performed over time.
