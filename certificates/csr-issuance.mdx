KryptoVault Certificate Signing Request (CSR) provides a centralized capability for generating CSRs and managing certificate issuance activity within Certificate Management.

It gives organizations a consistent place to create certificate requests, track their processing state, and maintain visibility into the resulting certificate lifecycle.

<Info>
  This page describes the CSR capability currently represented in KryptoVault. It does not assume certificate-authority behavior that is not defined in the available product sources.
</Info>

## CSR generation

KryptoVault supports the creation of Certificate Signing Requests as part of the certificate management process.

A CSR represents a certificate request before the final certificate is issued. Keeping CSR activity inside KryptoVault provides a central record of certificate requests and their current processing state.

## CSR lifecycle

The current KryptoVault interface represents CSR activity through a defined set of states.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Created">
    The CSR has been created and is present in the request lifecycle.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Validating">
    The CSR is in the validation stage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submitted">
    The CSR has moved into the submitted stage of processing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Signed">
    The CSR has reached the signed stage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Imported">
    The CSR has reached the imported stage and the resulting certificate can be represented within Certificate Management.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A CSR can also reach **Rejected** or **Cancelled** as a final processing outcome.

<Note>
  The CSR lifecycle represents the state of the certificate request. The resulting certificate is managed separately through Certificate Inventory and its certificate lifecycle.
</Note>

This gives certificate-management teams a consolidated view of request activity without requiring separate tracking outside KryptoVault.

## Certificate issuance continuity

CSR activity is connected with the broader certificate management lifecycle.

Once a CSR results in a certificate, the certificate can become part of the centralized Certificate Inventory, where it can participate in the certificate-management capabilities available in KryptoVault.

<Info>
  The available sources confirm CSR generation, CSR lifecycle visibility, and certificate issuance as product capabilities. Detailed CA routing, signing behavior, and issuer-specific CSR processing are intentionally not described here because those behaviors are not defined in the provided sources.
</Info>

## Governance and traceability

CSR activity is part of the KryptoVault certificate-management environment and can be governed through the platform's existing role and permission model.

Maintaining CSR state within the platform also provides operational traceability across certificate request activity and its eventual outcome.
