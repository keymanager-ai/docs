KryptoVault Certificate Stores provide a structured way to organize and assign certificates according to application, environment, ownership, or operational context.

They act as logical management containers within the certificate lifecycle, helping organizations keep certificate populations grouped in a way that reflects how infrastructure is actually managed.

<Info>
  A Certificate Store provides operational context for a certificate. It helps answer where the certificate belongs, who is responsible for it, and how it should be handled across lifecycle activities.
</Info>

## Organizing certificates

Certificate Stores allow organizations to group certificates into meaningful operational boundaries.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Application or Service" icon="apartment">
    Group certificates that belong to a specific application, service, or platform.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Environment" icon="layer-group">
    Separate certificate populations across environments such as production, staging, or development.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Business or Team Ownership" icon="users">
    Organize certificates according to the team, business unit, or operational owner responsible for them.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Deployment Context" icon="server">
    Associate certificates with the infrastructure or destination where they are managed or expected to be used.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

This model reduces fragmented certificate ownership and provides a consistent organizational structure across the certificate estate.

## Assigning certificates to stores

Certificates can be associated with a Certificate Store as they enter or move through the KryptoVault lifecycle.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Certificate enters KryptoVault">
    A certificate may arrive through discovery, import, enrollment, issuance, or renewal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Store context is applied">
    The certificate is associated with the Certificate Store that represents its intended operational or ownership context.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Lifecycle continues within that context">
    Renewal, reassignment, migration, and other lifecycle activities can retain the certificate's store relationship.
  </Step>
</Steps>

This helps KryptoVault maintain continuity between the certificate asset and the environment where it belongs.

## Store relationships

A Certificate Store represents the operational side of the certificate relationship, while the issuer represents the certificate's source of trust and issuance.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Issuer Relationship" icon="building-columns">
    Identifies where the certificate was issued and which CA or issuer signed it.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Store Relationship" icon="folder-tree">
    Identifies where the certificate is logically managed, grouped, or intended for use.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Together, these relationships provide both **issuance context** and **operational context** for a managed certificate.

## Multi-store certificate management

A certificate can be associated with more than one Certificate Store where the same certificate is relevant across multiple operational locations.

This is useful in environments such as high-availability services, clustered applications, redundant infrastructure, or multi-region deployments.

<Note>
  Multi-store relationships help teams understand the broader operational impact of a certificate change, renewal, or replacement.
</Note>

## Discovery integration

Certificate Stores are directly integrated with KryptoVault Discovery.

When automatic import is enabled, certificates identified through discovery can be associated with a selected Certificate Store as they enter the managed inventory.

This gives newly discovered certificates an immediate organizational context rather than leaving them as isolated inventory records.

<Card title="Discovery to Store" icon="radar">
  Discovery identifies the certificate, onboarding brings it into inventory, and the Certificate Store associates it with the correct operational context.
</Card>

## CSR and issuance integration

Certificates generated through CSR and issuance workflows can also be associated with the appropriate Certificate Store.

This ensures that newly issued certificates enter the managed lifecycle with a clear destination or ownership context from the beginning.

Certificate Stores therefore provide a common organizational model across certificates originating from discovery, import, enrollment, issuance, and renewal.

## Ownership and governance

Certificate Stores can be used as organizational boundaries for certificate ownership and responsibility.

They help separate certificate populations across teams, applications, environments, or business units and provide a clearer management context for renewal, remediation, and operational accountability.

Access to store management and certificate assignment can be controlled through KryptoVault's role-based access model so that sensitive organizational changes remain restricted to authorized users.

## Traceability

KryptoVault can maintain the relationship between certificates and their stores as certificate ownership or operational placement changes over time.

This traceability is useful during certificate renewal, infrastructure migration, ownership changes, compliance review, incident investigation, and environment decommissioning.
