KryptoVault Discovery identifies certificates deployed across network, domain, and supported cloud environments and brings them under centralized certificate management.

It helps organizations continuously detect certificates that may exist outside formal issuance workflows, reduce certificate blind spots, and keep the managed certificate inventory aligned with the actual infrastructure estate.

<Info>
  Discovery is designed as the entry point for certificates that already exist in the environment but were not necessarily issued through KryptoVault.
</Info>

## Discovery types

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Network Discovery">
    **Network Discovery** identifies certificates exposed across IP-based infrastructure.

    It supports discovery across individual IP addresses, CIDR-based networks, IP ranges, and selected service ports. This makes it suitable for internal infrastructure where certificates may be deployed across application servers, web servers, load balancers, gateways, network appliances, and other TLS-enabled endpoints.

    Network Discovery is intended for infrastructure-oriented certificate visibility where the location of the service is primarily known through its network address.

    Certificates identified through the scan can subsequently be brought into the managed inventory and associated with a certificate store.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Domain Discovery">
    **Domain Discovery** identifies certificates associated with configured domain names.

    It is suitable for websites, APIs, application endpoints, public-facing services, internal domain-based services, and other environments where certificate visibility is organized around DNS names rather than network ranges.

    Multiple domains can be assessed as part of a discovery scope, allowing organizations to monitor certificate deployments across broader application and service estates.

    The discovery process connects to the applicable services and retrieves the certificates presented by those endpoints.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Cloudflare Discovery">
    **Cloudflare Discovery** extends certificate visibility into environments managed through Cloudflare.

    KryptoVault connects to an authorized Cloudflare environment through API-based integration and retrieves certificate and related asset information available through that connection.

    This model is useful for organizations that manage internet-facing applications, domains, edge services, or TLS termination through Cloudflare.

    Certificates identified through Cloudflare Discovery can participate in the same centralized inventory and lifecycle model as certificates discovered through network or domain scanning.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Discovery jobs

A **Discovery Job** represents a reusable definition of what KryptoVault should discover and how the discovery operation should be executed.

Rather than treating each scan as an isolated activity, KryptoVault separates the persistent job configuration from individual executions of that job.

This allows the same discovery definition to be used repeatedly for on-demand discovery, scheduled execution, certificate inventory refresh, infrastructure monitoring, and periodic compliance assessment.

The job retains the discovery context while each execution is recorded separately through its run history.

<Tip>
  A well-defined discovery scope helps organizations progressively map their certificate estate instead of relying only on certificates known to PKI administrators.
</Tip>

## Discovery processing lifecycle

A discovery operation moves through several logical stages before identified certificates become managed assets.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Define discovery scope">
    KryptoVault receives the network, domain, or connected cloud environment that should be assessed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluate reachable services">
    The platform connects to the applicable endpoints and services within the configured scope.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify certificates">
    Certificates presented by reachable services are collected as discovery results.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Process execution results">
    Successful and unsuccessful portions of the discovery scope are recorded for the individual run.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Onboard certificates">
    Eligible discovered certificates can be imported into the managed certificate inventory.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply lifecycle management">
    Once managed, the certificates become available for expiration monitoring, trust analysis, cryptographic assessment, lifecycle tracking, and store association.
  </Step>
</Steps>

This creates a continuous flow from **infrastructure visibility** to **certificate governance**.

## Discovery Job Lifecycle

The persistent Discovery Job has its own lifecycle.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Created">
    A newly defined discovery job begins in the **Created** state. At this stage, the job exists as a reusable discovery definition and is available for execution.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scheduled">
    When an execution has been initiated or scheduled and is waiting to begin processing, the job moves to the **Scheduled** state.
  </Step>
  <Step title="In Progress">
    When KryptoVault begins processing the discovery scope, the job enters the **In Progress** state.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Completed">
    After successful execution, the job returns to the **Completed** state and remains available for future runs.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  The Discovery Job state represents the current operational condition of the reusable job. It is different from the outcome of an individual execution.
</Note>

## Discovery Run Lifecycle

Each time a Discovery Job executes, KryptoVault creates a separate **run**.

This allows one persistent Discovery Job to accumulate a complete execution history over time.

A run can originate from:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Manual Run" icon="play">
    An execution started on demand for immediate discovery.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Scheduled Run" icon="calendar-clock">
    An execution initiated automatically according to the job's configured schedule.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Run states

<Steps>
  <Step title="In Progress">
    The run is actively processing the configured discovery scope.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Success">
    The execution completed successfully and its discovery results are available.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fail">
    The execution could not complete successfully. Failure information is retained as part of the run history.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Run intelligence

Each discovery run provides execution-level intelligence about what occurred during that specific scan.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Execution Context" icon="clock-rotate-left">
    Provides historical visibility into when a run started, when it completed, how long it ran, and whether it was manual or scheduled.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Discovery Result" icon="chart-simple">
    Provides a summarized view of the discovery scope processed and the successful or failed portions of the execution.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

State transition history is retained for a run, allowing teams to understand how the execution progressed over time.

<Info>
  **Discovery Job** = reusable discovery definition and its current operational state.

  **Discovery Run** = one execution of that job and its individual result.
</Info>

## Certificate onboarding

Discovery does not end when a certificate is found.

KryptoVault can connect discovery directly with the managed certificate lifecycle by importing identified certificates into the centralized inventory.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Certificate discovered">
    KryptoVault identifies a certificate within the configured discovery scope.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Import decision">
    The discovery policy determines whether the certificate should be brought into managed inventory.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Store association">
    When automatic import is enabled, the certificate is associated with an appropriate Certificate Store.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Managed certificate">
    The certificate becomes available to the broader Certificate Management capabilities.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Once onboarded, a discovered certificate can participate in certificate lifecycle monitoring, expiration management, trust and chain assessment, issuer association, cryptographic analysis, risk assessment, and Certificate Store organization.

## Partial Result Handling

Discovery operations may include a large number of endpoints, and some portions of the scope may occasionally be unreachable.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Allow partial results">
    Successfully discovered certificates can be retained even if part of the configured discovery scope could not be completed.

    This model prioritizes maximum certificate visibility and allows usable discovery results to enter inventory despite isolated failures.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Require complete results">
    Discovery output can be withheld when part of the expected scope is missing.

    This model prioritizes completeness and is useful where organizations require the entire discovery scope to be processed successfully before accepting the results.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Operational Controls

Discovery can interact with production infrastructure, so KryptoVault provides controls around when and where scans can operate.

### Blackout Windows

Blackout windows prevent discovery jobs from running during restricted operational periods such as peak business hours, planned maintenance periods, sensitive infrastructure operations, or high-traffic processing windows.

Authorized users can be permitted to override the restriction for specific discovery activity when organizational policy allows it.

### Blacklist Governance

KryptoVault supports centralized blacklist policies for excluding infrastructure or certificate sources from discovery.

Blacklist policies can apply to areas such as domains, IP-based infrastructure, ports, and certificate issuers.

<Warning>
  Blacklist controls provide a governance boundary for discovery. Overrides should be restricted to trusted administrative roles.
</Warning>

### Timeout and Retry

Discovery can encounter endpoints that are temporarily unavailable or slow to respond.

Timeout and retry controls help prevent a single endpoint from indefinitely blocking a discovery operation while still allowing temporary connectivity failures to be retried.

This improves discovery resilience across distributed infrastructure.

## Governance and Traceability

Discovery operations are governed through KryptoVault's role-based access model.

Organizations can control access to sensitive discovery capabilities such as execution, configuration changes, automatic certificate onboarding, settings, and policy overrides.

KryptoVault also retains operational history around discovery jobs and runs, providing traceability for configuration changes, execution activity, outcomes, and state transitions.
